# Clinical Summary Safety Guidelines

## Interpreting Abnormal Lab Values

An abnormal lab value is any result outside the reference range for that
analyte, but not yet at a critical threshold. Abnormal glucose readings
(fasting glucose above 99 mg/dL) in a patient with known type 2 diabetes
mellitus warrant a review of current medication dosing, dietary adherence, and
recent illness or medication changes before adjusting therapy. Mild
electrolyte abnormalities (potassium, sodium) that remain within one unit of
the reference range are typically monitored with a repeat draw within one to
two weeks rather than treated acutely. Persistent or worsening trends across
multiple visits should prompt closer follow-up even if no single value is
critical.

## When to Escalate to a Clinician

Any critical lab value — one that meets or exceeds a defined critical
threshold — requires immediate clinician notification and should never wait
for a routine follow-up visit. Examples include severe hyperkalemia
(potassium at or above 6.0 mmol/L), severe hypoglycemia, or acute kidney
injury markers rising sharply from baseline. For abnormal-but-not-critical
findings, escalate sooner than a standard interval if the patient reports new
or worsening symptoms (e.g., increased thirst, fatigue, palpitations,
shortness of breath) that correlate with the abnormal value, or if the patient
has comorbidities (chronic kidney disease, cardiac disease) that reduce their
tolerance for the abnormality.

## Condition-Specific Follow-Up Actions

For type 2 diabetes mellitus with elevated glucose above the reference range,
recheck fasting glucose and HbA1c within 1-2 weeks, and review metformin
dosing and adherence before escalating to additional therapy.

For mild hypokalemia in a patient on a thiazide diuretic such as
hydrochlorothiazide, recheck serum potassium within 1-2 weeks, provide
dietary potassium counseling, and consider reviewing the diuretic dose if the
abnormality persists.

For suspected iron-deficiency anemia with low hemoglobin, order iron studies
(ferritin, iron, and total iron-binding capacity), recheck hemoglobin in
approximately 4 weeks, and monitor for worsening symptoms such as increasing
fatigue or shortness of breath.

For chronic kidney disease with mildly elevated creatinine, continue current
chronic kidney disease management, recheck creatinine and eGFR at the next
scheduled interval, and monitor for any acute worsening relative to the
patient's baseline.

For suspected hypothyroidism with elevated TSH, order a free T4 level and
repeat TSH in 4-6 weeks, and consider an endocrinology referral if the
diagnosis is confirmed.

## Documentation Standards for Summaries

Generated summaries must restate only what is present in the source report and
the deterministic findings supplied alongside it. Numeric values and units
must be preserved exactly as recorded. Any recommendation for follow-up
testing or medication review should reference the specific abnormal finding it
responds to, so the treating clinician can trace the recommendation back to
its supporting evidence.
